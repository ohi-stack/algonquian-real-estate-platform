# ARE Customer Voice & Search Intent Standard

**Owner:** Algonquian Real Estate LLC  
**Applies to:** Public website pages, service pages, landing pages, SEO content, public plugin interfaces, intake flows, automated customer communications, FAQs, CTAs, and customer-facing agent output.

## Governing Principle

Algonquian Real Estate organizes operations internally around services, workflows, plugins, and record ownership. Public communication must be organized around the customer's **situation, question, problem, and desired outcome**.

> **Internal structure: what ARE offers. External language: what the customer needs.**

A customer should be able to recognize their situation before they need to understand ARE's formal service name.

## Required Customer-Facing Sequence

Every customer-facing page or interaction should answer these questions in order:

1. **Is this for someone like me?** — name the customer's situation in plain language.
2. **Do you understand what I am dealing with?** — identify the practical pressures, uncertainty, timing, property condition, money, distance, family, or maintenance issues that commonly drive the need.
3. **What are my options?** — explain legitimate paths without forcing a sale or a single service outcome.
4. **How can Algonquian Real Estate help?** — introduce the formal service only after the customer's need is clear.
5. **What happens next?** — explain the next steps and what the customer should expect.
6. **What should I do now?** — give one primary action-oriented CTA.

## Copy Rules

### Lead with customer language

Prefer:

- "I inherited a house. What do I do with it?"
- "How can I sell my house without making repairs?"
- "Who can keep an eye on my property while I am away?"
- "My house is too much to maintain. What are my options?"
- "I am not ready to sell yet, but I need help with the property."
- "Can I sell my property and receive payments over time?"
- "I have a property or deal. Is Algonquian interested in it?"
- "How can I invest in or lend on Algonquian real estate opportunities?"

Avoid leading with internal labels such as "Estate Transition Assistance," "Acquisition Submission," or "Property Stewardship" without first explaining the customer problem those services solve.

### Formal service names remain authoritative

Formal names remain valid for:

- navigation and information architecture;
- product/service records;
- pricing and contracts;
- plugin and workflow ownership;
- internal reporting;
- structured documentation; and
- compliance records.

The public page may retain the formal service title, but the hero, supporting copy, FAQ, SEO cluster, and CTA language should use customer-intent wording.

## SEO and Content Cluster Rule

Formal service pages are hub pages. Supporting content should be written around actual customer questions and search intent.

Example — **Inherited Property Guidance** hub:

- I Inherited a House — What Should I Do?
- Should I Keep or Sell an Inherited House?
- Can I Sell an Inherited House As-Is?
- What If the Inherited House Needs Major Repairs?
- What If I Live Out of State and Inherited a Connecticut Property?
- How Do I Handle a House Full of Belongings After Someone Dies?

Each supporting page should route to the correct formal service and intake pathway without overstating ARE's legal, tax, probate, fiduciary, licensed-inspection, or financial role.

## CTA Standard

Prefer customer-action CTAs:

- Tell Us What's Going On
- See My Options
- Get My Property Reviewed
- I Need Help With a Property
- See If You'd Buy My Property
- Talk Through My Situation
- Submit My Property
- Request Property Support
- Join the Buyer Network
- Discuss a Lending Relationship

Avoid generic CTAs when a more specific customer action is possible, including "Learn More," "Services," or "Solutions."

## Application and Plugin UI Requirement

Public shortcodes and customer-facing plugin states must follow this standard. The interface must explain the user's next step in plain language and must not expose internal plugin terminology as the primary customer experience.

Examples:

- `[algq_homeowner_options]` should frame choices around the owner's situation before naming service categories.
- `[algq_deal_intake_form]` should explain why the information is requested and what happens after submission.
- buyer registration/login/marketplace surfaces should explain the access path from the buyer's perspective.
- `[algq_stewardship_portal]` should use client-facing property language rather than system jargon.

## Automation and Agent Requirement

Any ARE automation or agent producing customer-facing text must:

1. identify the customer's situation;
2. use the customer's vocabulary where reasonably known;
3. avoid internal plugin, state-machine, or database terminology in customer copy;
4. present options accurately without coercion;
5. preserve legal and professional-scope boundaries;
6. state the next action clearly; and
7. log the canonical service/workflow internally without forcing that terminology into the customer message.

## Acceptance Criteria

A customer-facing page or message passes this standard when a first-time visitor can answer, without understanding ARE's internal architecture:

- "Does this apply to me?"
- "Does ARE understand the problem I have?"
- "What choices do I have?"
- "How can ARE help?"
- "What happens next?"
- "What do I click or do now?"

If the content begins with ARE's internal service taxonomy and requires the customer to translate it into their own situation, it fails this standard.
